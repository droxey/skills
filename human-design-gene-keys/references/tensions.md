# Tensions protocol

Use this protocol with the [research workflow](research-workflow.md) when the request concerns competing impulses, contradictions, repeated patterns, or tensions across a supplied chart/profile.

## Choose an entry point

An explicit request to discover tensions starts with the chart, not a forced personality questionnaire. An explicit lived situation starts with that situation. When neither is clear, ask one short question:

> Should I discover possible tensions from the chart first, or start with a situation where you feel pulled in two directions?

Each candidate needs a counterexample rather than only confirming examples.

Do not preload another person's tensions or require agreement with examples. A response of “none of these” changes the working hypothesis, not the person's credibility.

## Build candidates

Read the available record before generating candidates. Research each side through the primary sources specified in the workflow. Consider structural context, conscious versus unconscious activations, repeated Keys, sequence-specific line meanings, and differences between sourced descriptions without assuming they must oppose one another.

For each candidate, identify two chart features and the source support for both. Explain why their proposed expressions could compete in a particular situation. Two different keywords alone do not establish a contradiction.

Keep these categories separate:

| Category | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Possible personal tension | Two verified features and sourced teachings suggest a situational pull; label the connection interpretive |
| Source disagreement | Two reports or teachings make incompatible claims; name the disagreement and do not project it onto the subject |
| Data conflict | Chart values differ; preserve both and resolve controlling inputs before line-specific claims |
| Vocabulary mismatch | Similar terms mean different things across systems, spheres, or providers; explain rather than merge |
| Unsupported association | There is insufficient support for one side or the relationship; omit or label unverified |

Do not count the same activation appearing in several Gene Keys sequences as independent corroboration. Do not infer a complete channel from an associated channel listing. Do not claim statistical rarity without a suitable dataset and reproducible comparison.

## Rank and present

Show up to three candidates, ranked by source support and relevance to the request. State when fewer are supported. Use a compact record for each:

- **Possible tension:** a neutral phrase describing both sides.
- **Evidence:** both chart features, source locations, and the relevant teachings.
- **Hypothesis:** one concrete situation where both needs might be reasonable.
- **Counterexample:** what would suggest this does not fit, plus an ordinary non-chart explanation.
- **Practice:** a small way to accommodate both sides without treating either as a defect.

A tension need not be solved by suppressing one side. Context, timing, communication, role boundaries, or ordinary constraints may explain the apparent opposition. Clearly label these practical suggestions as synthesis rather than canonical teachings.

## Interview and reading boundary

During discovery, ask which candidate fits or invite “these do not fit.” Ask only one current question, with no demand for trauma disclosure. Continue from the reply instead of restarting the full intake.

When the user requested a complete reading rather than an interview, include three reflection questions and select one small experiment as specified in the main workflow. End with the experiment. Do not turn a reflection into an unrequested calendar task, reminder, diagnosis, career prescription, or relationship verdict.
